Rodent Control in Fort Worth: A Seasonal Guide for Texas Homeowners

Rodent pressure in Fort Worth is not one problem but four - one per season - and the homeowners who stay rodent-free are the ones whose prevention follows the calendar instead of the scratching sounds.

Spring is breeding season: colonies expand, and last winter's single entry point becomes this spring's nursery. Summer pushes rodents toward water and shade - irrigation lines, pool equipment and dense ground cover near the foundation. Fall is the great move indoors, when cooling nights and the acorn drop send roof rats looking for attic housing. Winter is when the damage compounds quietly in insulation while everyone assumes the problem ended with the cold.

The work changes with the season too. Spring is for inspection and trapping down young colonies before they establish. Summer is perimeter work - stations checked, harborage cut back, water leaks fixed. Fall is sealing season: steel mesh at the soffits, ridge vents and weep holes before the first cold front. Winter is monitoring - the stations and a mid-season attic check that catches the one entry everyone missed.

None of this needs to be complicated, but it does need to be in the right order and on the right dates. A Fort Worth home on a seasonal rhythm - inspect, remove, seal, monitor - rarely makes the emergency call at all.
